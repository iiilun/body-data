## Selection and comparability

- Country comparisons are separate observations; no unweighted average of national percentages is invented. All percentage values are on a 0–100 scale. Missing World Bank values are excluded, never changed to zero.
- Emissions, national drinking-water access and national Internet access use common year 2024. National unpaid-care surveys keep their actual years and footnotes. A country time-use ranking is not a synchronized global experiment.
- SHELTER emissions are annual territorial CO₂, excluding LULUCF, and are not all greenhouse gases. WMO temperature is a global annual anomaly; ILO work-heat effects are modelled for 2020.
- SPACE national slum percentages use urban population as denominator. Slum residence, homelessness, inadequate housing and Taiwan model mortgage burden are different concepts. The Taiwan percentage is not the share of real households overburdened.
- THIRST service-ladder categories are nested under the lack-of-safely-managed total; never add the total to the components. The global access percentage supports the rain ratio and is the same broad service-gap observation, not an extra independent population count.
- OFFLINE global observations are 2026 estimates. The country comparison is 2024. Internet use, mobile coverage and data affordability have different denominators and cannot be added.
- VACANT has no World vacant-dwelling total. Its World cards supply housing-deprivation context measured in people. Country vacancy definitions and years differ; the dataset is a comparison of observations, not a harmonized vacancy league table. `subsetOf` denotes nested totals/subgroups. Never add a parent to its children. Counts and percentages of one fact have not been duplicated as separate national cards.

## Interactive meaning

WHO recommends at least 15 litres per person per day as soon as possible during an emergency. This includes basic water needs; it is not a child’s drinking-water requirement or an ordinary adequate daily consumption target. WHO also notes about 20 litres for basic personal and food hygiene, and potentially more for laundry/bathing. The proposed conversion `illustrative safely supplied litres / 15` therefore represents **emergency basic-water person-days**, with the simulated-litre premise visible in the artwork’s data explanation. [WHO guidance](https://www.who.int/teams/environment-climate-change-and-health/water-sanitation-and-health/environmental-health-in-emergencies/humanitarian-emergencies)

For the water visualization, use blue for the safely-managed service share and ochre for **service safety not assured**. Not meeting the full safely-managed criteria can reflect distance, availability or unverified quality; it does not establish that every drop is contaminated. WHO/JMP population coverage is a metaphorical interaction weight, not a measured probability that an individual captured drop is safe.

For VACANT, official vacancy categories support the idea that existing stock can be inaccessible: Japan separates rental, sale, secondary and other vacant homes; Ireland records renovation, rental transitions and other reasons. None establishes a universal immediately accessible percentage. `accessibleShare` and `lockedShare` intentionally remain null; any locks are an artistic rule or clearly named national category, never an invented real-world rate. Do not present successful movements as real rehousing.

## Housing-need context update (22 November 2026)

SPACE replaces the Canada and Spain slum-percentage cards with two primary-report estimates. VACANT replaces the Vigo and A Coruña vacancy-percentage cards with global housing-inadequacy and slum-resident context. All scenes remain at 24 cards; 144 total. VACANT opens with Japan’s 9.002 million vacant dwellings, then the global housing-inadequacy upper estimate, then global slum residents. These are separate geographic scopes, reference periods and units; there is no direct conversion to rehousing capacity.

- **Up to 3.4 billion people without secure, safe and adequate housing:** verified in [UN-Habitat World Cities Report 2026, full PDF](https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/2026/05/wcr_2026.pdf), Executive Director introduction p.iii / PDF p.5 and key findings p.xv / PDF p.17. This is a broad estimate incorporating multiple forms of deprivation and underlying dates, **not a 2026 world census, a homelessness count, or a shortage expressed in dwellings**. The displayed year is the report year. All seven labels retain the upper-bound qualifier. The report’s introduction/key findings say “up to”; Chapter 1’s service-based discussion also uses “at least” for 3.4bn, illustrating heterogeneous scope and evidence. This artwork deliberately preserves the headline’s upper-bound wording instead of presenting an exact unqualified total.
- **At least 64 million informal-settlement evictions/displacements during 2003–2023:** verified in the same PDF, key findings p.xix / PDF p.21, pp.87 and 89, and Box 4.3 p.97 / PDF p.127. Box 4.3 combines satellite imagery with local enumerations across 94 cities in Africa, Asia and Latin America, representing 14% of urban population in less developed regions, then extrapolates a weighted/averaged global estimate. It is a cumulative estimate for urban-development displacement from informal settlements with incomplete monitoring; it is not all forms of eviction, an annual count or a present displacement stock. The report’s “at least” qualifier is preserved in every language.
- **1.16 billion slum/informal-settlement residents, 2024:** the existing [UN-Habitat / UNSD SDG Report 2026 Goal 11](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/report/2026/Goal-11) observation is reused as a VACANT contextual card with a new scene/id. It is **not** added to the broad inadequacy estimate, which already includes overlapping housing deprivations.

Primary publication landing page: [World Cities Report 2026](https://unhabitat.org/knowledge/publications/world-cities-report-2026-the-global-housing-crisis-pathways-to-action). Its summary also reports the 3.4bn and approximately 64mn figures; qualifiers and the more precise eviction scope above come from the full report. [WHO, 31 October 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/commentaries/detail/on-world-cities-day-2026--who-calls-for-safe--healthy-housing-for-all) independently repeats the report’s up-to-3.4bn headline. No world vacant-home total is inferred.
